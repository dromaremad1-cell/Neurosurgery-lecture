Complete Theory

Source Content Definition

Entrapment neuropathy occurs when a nerve passes through a tight space or tunnel formed by ligaments, bones and/or muscles, usually near joints. Nerve damage occurs through compression and ischemia.

Source Content Carpal Tunnel Syndrome — Definition & Predisposition

Median nerve compression beneath the transverse carpal ligament/flexor retinaculum. Source states ~1% prevalence and more frequent in females. Predisposing factors listed: ligamentous/synovial thickening, trauma, obesity, diabetes, thyroid disease, amyloidosis, gout, acromegaly, Paget’s disease, and mucopolysaccharidoses.

Source Content Carpal Tunnel — Clinical Picture & Tests

Pain and intermittent numbness in lateral 3½ fingers, often worse at night; weakness of thenar muscles and lateral two lumbricals. Tinel's test: percussion over median nerve reproduces paresthesia. Compression test: symptoms reproduced after 30 s pressure over carpal tunnel. Phalen: full wrist flexion for 60 s reproduces symptoms; source gives sensitivity 71%, specificity 80%.

Source Content Carpal Tunnel — Investigations, DDx & Treatment

Nerve conduction is the most sensitive physiological diagnostic technique and shows delayed sensory/motor conduction. Differential diagnosis: C6/C7 cervical radiculopathy, thoracic outlet syndrome, pronator teres syndrome, anterior interosseous syndrome (pinch-sign weakness without sensory loss). Treatment in source: treat systemic disease, reduce hand use, NSAID, division of transverse carpal ligament.

Source Content Cubital Tunnel Syndrome

Ulnar nerve entrapment at the elbow within cubital tunnel. Elbow flexion narrows the tunnel; extension widens it. Symptoms: paresthesia/numbness/pain over palmar and dorsal ulnar hand and 4th/5th fingers, sometimes provoked by prolonged flexion. Weakness starts in first dorsal interosseous and may progress to hypothenar/intrinsic hand wasting; sensory symptoms usually precede weakness; Tinel may be present.

Source Content Cubital Tunnel — Investigations, DDx & Treatment

Motor nerve conduction shows delayed velocity. DDx: C8-T1 radiculopathy, thoracic outlet, syringomyelia (dissociated sensory loss, long-tract signs, MRI diagnostic), motor neuron disease (wasting/fasciculation without sensory loss), and ulnar nerve entrapment at the wrist/hand (Guyon’s canal). In the source, Guyon’s canal sensory loss involves the medial 4th/5th fingers while the palmar and dorsal surfaces of the hand are spared because sensory branches arise proximal to the wrist; weakness predominantly affects ulnar-innervated thenar muscles relative to hypothenar muscles. Treatment: neurolysis/decompression ± anterior ulnar nerve transposition.

Source Content Thoracic Outlet Syndrome

Neurogenic thoracic outlet syndrome = brachial plexus compression at thoracic outlet. Causes: scalene muscles, fibrous bands, cervical rib, elongated cervical transverse processes. Symptoms: supraclavicular pain, arm pain/paresthesia involving 4th/5th fingers, possible decreased radial pulse, edema, cyanosis. Signs: supraclavicular tenderness; source describes Adson's sign with shoulder abduction/external rotation producing symptoms. Investigations: X-ray for cervical rib, ENG/EMG, subclavian angiography. Treatment in young patients with cervical rib/long process: exploration and removal or fibrous-band section.

Source Content Meralgia Paresthetica

Entrapment of lateral cutaneous nerve under lateral inguinal ligament supplying anterolateral thigh. Causes: obesity with constricting garments, abdominal distension (ascites/pregnancy/tumor), iliac crest graft too near ASIS. Clinical picture: burning dysesthesia and hypesthesia over anterolateral thigh, worse standing/walking. Treatment: weight reduction, NSAID, surgical neurolysis/decompression.

Source Content Tarsal Tunnel Syndrome

Compression of posterior tibial nerve behind medial malleolus beneath the laciniate ligament. Causes: local fractures, tumors, vascular abnormalities. Clinical picture: pain/dysesthesia in toes/sole, pes cavus and clawing of toes, positive Tinel below medial malleolus. Treatment: NSAID, ankle support/orthoses; source notes surgical release is less rewarding than in carpal tunnel.

High-Yield Summary

Entrapment neuropathy

Nerve compression in a tight anatomical tunnel causing ischemic injury.

CTS

Median nerve under flexor retinaculum; nocturnal lateral 3½-finger symptoms + thenar weakness; Tinel/compression/Phalen; NCS most sensitive physiological test.

Cubital tunnel

Ulnar nerve at elbow; 4th/5th-finger sensory symptoms, first dorsal interosseous weakness, intrinsic hand wasting.

Thoracic outlet

Brachial plexus compression from scalene/fibrous band/cervical rib/elongated transverse process.

Meralgia

Lateral cutaneous nerve; anterolateral-thigh burning/hypesthesia.

Tarsal tunnel

Posterior tibial nerve behind medial malleolus; sole/toe pain + Tinel.
