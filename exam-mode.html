01

Congenital CNS — Hydrocephalus & Craniosynostosis

● Source Content5-Minute Recall

5 Minutes Before Exam

Ultra-fast recall of the highest-yield facts from the uploaded lecture.

Hydrocephalus = increased CSF volume inside ventricles ± increased ICP.
CSF route: Lateral → Monroe → 3rd → Sylvius → 4th → Magendi/Luschka → subarachnoid space → arachnoid villi/granulations → superior sagittal sinus.
Mechanisms: obstruction / malabsorption / hypersecretion. Mass obstruction = tumor/hemorrhage. Flow obstruction = ↑ protein viscosity. Malabsorption = superior sagittal sinus thrombosis/obstruction. Hypersecretion = choroid plexus hyperplasia/papilloma.
Infantile hydrocephalus: macrocephaly + separated sutures + tense bulging fontanelle + dilated scalp veins + sun-set eyes/Parinaud + VI palsy + seizures + delayed milestones.
Parinaud / sun-set sign: pressure of floor of dilated 3rd ventricle on tectum of midbrain.
Adult hydrocephalus: morning headache, vomiting, blurred vision, bilateral papilledema, diplopia/squint from VI palsy, impaired consciousness.
NPH (Hakim Adam): elderly + dilated ventricles + normal ICP + triad: dementia, gait disturbance, urinary incontinence.
IIH / pseudotumor cerebri: raised ICP symptoms + no ventricular dilatation + high LP pressure + papilledema.
Imaging: CT/MRI show dilated ventricles and cause of obstruction. Maternal ultrasound may detect enlarged head / hydrocephalus antenatally.
Treatment: treat cause + divert CSF. VP = most commonly used. VA = most physiological. ETV = 3rd ventricle → prepontine cistern. LP = lumbar cistern → peritoneum.
Shunt complications: infection (ventriculitis / peritonitis / endocarditis), obstruction, disconnection/migration/intestinal perforation, over-drainage (slit ventricle / subdural hematoma / craniostenosis).
Craniostenosis names:
Metopic → Trigonocephaly
Sagittal → Scaphocephaly (most common)
Unicoronal → Anterior Plagiocephaly + harlequin eye
Bicoronal → Brachycephaly + bilateral harlequin configuration
Lambdoid → Posterior Plagiocephaly
Macrocrania D.D.: familial, hydrocephalus, rickets, arachnoid cyst, infantile chronic subdural hematoma.
Source limitation: the uploaded file gives “Pansynostosis (Craniostenosis)” as a heading only, without explanatory text; no extra description has been added here.
02

Congenital CNS — Dysraphism / Myelomeningocele / Infantile SDH

● Source Content5-Minute Recall

5 Minutes Before Exam

Ultra-fast recall of the highest-yield facts from the uploaded lecture.

Craniosynostosis rule: fused suture blocks growth perpendicular to it → compensatory growth parallel to it.
Names: Metopic→Trigonocephaly; Sagittal→Scaphocephaly (most common); Unicoronal→Anterior Plagiocephaly + harlequin eye; Bicoronal→Brachycephaly; Lambdoid→Posterior Plagiocephaly; Pansynostosis→Oxycephaly.
Pansynostosis: towering skull + marked ↑ intracranial tension. Neurological problems are more common when ≥2 sutures fuse.
Imaging: skull X-ray shows fusion/deformity; 3-D CT shows thumbprint appearance; CT/MRI for coronal/metopic/lambdoid synostosis. Copper-beaten appearance is shown in the source X-ray.
Treatment craniosynostosis: suturectomy + cranial remodeling + facial advancement if proptosis.
Myelomeningocele: neural tissue + CSF, defective/placode covering, poor/partial transillumination, partial compression, neuro deficits, hydrocephalus may coexist, bad prognosis.
Meningocele: CSF only, intact normal skin, soft, totally/easily compressible, brilliant/total transillumination, neurology usually intact, good prognosis.
Myelomeningocele investigations: fetal US; whole-spine MRI; brain CT for associated hydrocephalus. D.D.: meningocele, lipomeningomyelocele, lumbosacral lipoma, sacrococcygeal teratoma.
Myelomeningocele treatment: folic acid prevention; excise sac + untether cord + repair dysraphic defects.
Rachischisis: exposed neural tissue, no sac, absent meninges, CSF leak.
Spina bifida occulta: intact skin ± tuft of hair/blue nevus; lamina defect; may be incidental/nocturnal enuresis → observation.
Diastematomyelia: split cord + bony spur → excise spur.
Tethered cord: low cord below L1–L2; sphincteric disturbance + progressive motor/sensory deficit + back pain + scoliosis → divide filum.
Encephalocele: CNS structures herniate through cranial defect; cranial meningocele or meningoencephalocele.
Infantile subdural hematoma: birth trauma common; 10% battered children; 85% bilateral/frontoparietal; large head + irritability/failure to thrive; diagnose by CT.
03

Head Trauma — Part 1

Source Content

5 Minutes Before Exam

GCS: 13–15 minor • 9–12 moderate • 3–8 severe
ABC: Airway first → oxygen/ventilation → circulation with 2 IV lines + urine output + fluids/blood as needed
Steroids: Contraindicated
ICP drugs: Mannitol + hypertonic saline + frusemide; elevate head of bed
Linear fracture: Most common skull fracture; no surgery
Basal fracture: Most serious skull fracture; may cause CSF fistula + cranial nerve injury
Anterior fossa: Raccoon eyes + CSF rhinorrhea + epistaxis + olfactory injury
Middle fossa: Bleeding per ear + CSF otorrhea + VII/VIII injury
Posterior fossa: Battle's sign + lower CN injury + brain-stem injury + atlantoaxial subluxation
Central tentorial: Consciousness worsens + DI + pupils small then fixed/dilated
Tonsillar: Cushing triad = hypertension + bradycardia + Cheyne-Stokes breathing
Concussion: Temporary loss of brain function; most common TBI; monitor + rest; confusion/amnesia usually improve within 3 weeks
04

Head Trauma — Part 2

Source Content

5 Minutes Before Exam

Concussion: Temporary dysfunction; most common TBI; monitor + rest; confusion/amnesia usually improve within 3 weeks.
Repeated concussion: Dementia pugilistica / Second-impact syndrome.
DAI: White matter tracts; about half of severe head trauma; MRI best.
Contusion: 20–30% of severe TBI; microhemorrhages; pia-arachnoid intact.
Edema: Early = minutes; delayed peak = 3–5 days.
EDH: 90% fissure fracture; MMA; 75% temporal; concussion → lucid interval → compression.
EDH CT: Hyperdense biconvex.
Uncal signs: Ipsilateral fixed pupil + gaze deviation; contralateral weakness. Ipsilateral weakness = Kernohan sign.
Acute SDH: <3 days; hyperdense concave; >80% skull fracture.
Subacute SDH: 4–21 days; isodense.
Chronic SDH: >21 days; bridging vein; elderly/atrophic/anticoagulant; 25% bilateral; hypodense.
Chronic SDH treatment: Burr holes + drain + postoperative hydration; conservative if mild and intact.
ICH surgery: Large superficial + mass effect/raised ICP OR posterior fossa.
SAH: Conservative; may later cause post-hemorrhagic hydrocephalus.
05

Brain Tumors

5 Minutes Before Exam

Raised ICP: Early-morning dull headache, worse with cough/strain; projectile vomiting; papilledema; VI palsy.
Foster-Kennedy: Ipsilateral anosmia + optic atrophy + contralateral papilledema.
Pineal: Parinaud sign + obstructive hydrocephalus.
Pituitary: Endocrine disturbance + bitemporal hemianopia.
CPA: VII/VIII involvement + tinnitus + cerebellar/brainstem signs.
Herniation: Ipsilateral III palsy + contralateral hemiparesis ± coma/apnea/cardiac arrest.
MRI: Gold standard.
Tumor apoplexy: Pituitary adenoma, meningioma, melanoma metastasis.
06

Cerebrovascular Diseases

5 Minutes Before Exam

Stroke mix: Ischemic 85% / hemorrhagic 15% in source.
TIA: Reversible ischemic event with complete recovery within 24 h — source definition.
Lacune: Small perforator infarct; internal capsule can cause major hemiparesis.
Territories: Carotid • vertebrobasilar • watershed.
4 Ps: Parenchyma • Pipes • Perfusion • Penumbra.
Thrombectomy: Source: standard for large-vessel MCA/ICA occlusion in first 6 h with penumbra.
Malignant infarct: May require decompressive craniotomy/craniectomy.
07

Spontaneous Hemorrhage + Aneurysm + AVM

5 Minutes Before Exam

Deep ICH: Suggests hypertension in source.
ICH distribution: Putamen 35% • lobar 25% • thalamic 20% • pontine 10% • cerebellar 10%.
Cerebellar hematoma: Urgent posterior-fossa evacuation may be lifesaving.
SAH hallmark: Sudden severe headache + meningism ± LOC/seizures.
SAH complications: Rebleeding • hydrocephalus • vasospasm • seizures • cardiac dysfunction • pulmonary edema.
DSA: Gold-standard vascular study in source.
Nimodipine: Listed for vasospasm prevention.
AVM: Direct arterial-to-venous shunt without capillary network; common hemorrhagic cause in young adults.
08

Lumbar Spine Disorders

5 Minutes Before Exam

L4-L5: Compresses L5 → dorsiflexor weakness + lateral leg/dorsum foot/big toe sensory loss.
L5-S1: Compresses S1 → plantar-flexor weakness + posterior calf/lateral foot sensory loss + ankle jerk ↓.
SLRT: Positive if sciatica at <60°; useful for lower lumbar disc prolapse.
Reverse SLRT: Anterior-thigh pain; upper lumbar roots L2-L4.
Cauda equina: Urinary retention with overflow + saddle hypesthesia + bilateral LMN weakness = urgent surgery.
MRI: Best diagnostic test for lumbar disc prolapse in source.
Lumbar stenosis: Neurogenic claudication relieved by sitting/lying; SLRT often negative.
09

Spondylolisthesis + Spinal Cord Compression

5 Minutes Before Exam

Spondylolisthesis grades: G1 <25% • G2 25–50% • G3 50–75% • G4 >75%.
Above L1: Cord compression → UMN pattern.
Below L1: Cauda equina/root compression → LMN pattern.
Compression location: Extradural 80% • intradural extramedullary 15% • intradural intramedullary 5%.
Central cord: Upper limbs > lower; proximal > distal; dissociated sensory loss.
Brown-Séquard: Ipsilateral UMN + deep-sensory loss below; contralateral pain/temp loss.
Spinal shock: Hypotension + bradycardia; distinguish from hypovolemic shock with tachycardia.
Emergency: Spinal cord compression = neurosurgical emergency.
10

Cervical Disc Prolapse + Cervical Spondylosis

5 Minutes Before Exam

Common levels: C5-C6 and C6-C7.
Posterolateral: Radiculopathy.
Central: Myelopathy.
C5-C6: Usually compresses C6 root.
C6-C7: Usually compresses C7 root.
Myelopathy: Spastic quadriparesis + hyperreflexia + gait clumsiness + sphincter disturbance.
MRI: Best diagnostic technique for cervical disc prolapse.
CT: Best for bony details, stenosis and OPLL.
Surgery: Indicated for refractory brachialgia, persistent root deficit or myelopathy.
11

Spinal Trauma

5 Minutes Before Exam

Most common cause: Road traffic accident.
Denis model: Anterior • middle • posterior columns.
Stable: One-column injury.
Unstable: >2 columns disturbed; burst/translation/distraction patterns often unstable.
Burst fracture: Vertical compression; anterior + middle columns; highly unstable.
Incomplete injury: Residual motor/sensory function below; sacral sparing is key.
CT: Best for fracture and displaced bone fragments.
MRI: Best for cord contusion/compression.
Surgery: Unstable fracture even without deficit, or stable fracture with neural compression.
12

Entrapment Neuropathies

5 Minutes Before Exam

CTS: Median nerve under flexor retinaculum; nocturnal numbness lateral 3½ fingers.
Phalen: Full wrist flexion for 60 s reproduces symptoms.
NCS: Most sensitive physiological test for CTS in source.
Cubital tunnel: Ulnar nerve at elbow; 4th/5th fingers + intrinsic hand weakness.
Thoracic outlet: Scalene/fibrous band/cervical rib/elongated transverse process.
Meralgia: Lateral cutaneous nerve → anterolateral thigh burning/hypesthesia.
Tarsal tunnel: Posterior tibial nerve behind medial malleolus; sole/toe symptoms + Tinel.
13

Traumatic Nerve Injuries

5 Minutes Before Exam

Neuropraxia: Transient block; motor > sensory; recovery 24–48 h in source.
Axonotmesis: Axonal disruption with epineurium/perineurium intact; spontaneous regeneration may occur.
Neurotmesis: Complete division + Wallerian degeneration; no recovery without repair.
Erb: C5-C6 upper plexus → waiter's-tip deformity.
Klumpke: Lower plexus → intrinsic hand weakness ± Horner syndrome.
Radial: Wrist drop.
Median: Ape hand / pointing index / failed opposition.
Ulnar: Partial claw hand + Froment + interosseous wasting.
Common peroneal: Fibular-neck injury → foot drop.
Repair: Primary suture clean cut; secondary 3–6 weeks if crushed/contaminated.
