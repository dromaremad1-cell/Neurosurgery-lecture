Complete Theory

Source Content Overview & Epidemiology

Cerebrovascular disorders are grouped into cerebrovascular events and cerebrovascular diseases. Events include ischemic stroke (85%) and hemorrhagic stroke (15%). The source lists cerebral atherosclerosis, aneurysms, vascular malformations, blood disorders, Moyamoya and vasculitis among cerebrovascular diseases.

Epidemiology in the source: second most common cause of death, most common disabling neurological disorder, incidence increases with age, and is higher in men.

Source Content Risk Factors

Hypertension, heart disease, diabetes mellitus, smoking, hyperlipidemia, family history of vascular disease, obesity, lack of exercise and excessive alcohol consumption.

Source Content Ischemic Types

TIA: reversible ischemic event with complete recovery within 24 hours (source wording). Established brain infarct: persistent marked neurological deficit.

Source Content Ischemic Etiology

Occlusive: large-vessel atherosclerosis, perforating-artery disease/lacunar infarction. Embolic: cardioembolism plus fat, air and tumor emboli. Systemic arterial hypoperfusion.

Source Content Pathogenesis

Atheroma develops over months/years from subintimal lipid deposition, smooth-muscle proliferation and fibrosis. Plaque may narrow/occlude or ulcerate, triggering thrombosis or distal embolization. Large-artery infarction may become a malignant infarction. Small perforator/lenticulostriate infarcts are lacunar and can cause major deficits if strategically located, e.g. internal capsule.

Source Content Clinical Presentation & Territories

Sudden headache, focal neurological deficits related to vascular territory, impaired/lost consciousness, seizures. Territory patterns: carotid, vertebrobasilar and watershed.

Source Content Diagnosis & Investigations

History/exam identify affected arterial territory, attack frequency, cardiac disease and risk factors. Investigations include laboratory tests, chest X-ray, ECG, echocardiography, CT/CTA, MRI/MRA, digital subtraction angiography, carotid/transcranial Doppler and functional imaging.

Source Content 4 Ps of Acute Infarct Imaging

Parenchyma: early ischemic signs and exclude hemorrhage. Pipes: extracranial/intracranial circulation and thrombus. Perfusion: blood volume, blood flow, mean transit time. Penumbra: tissue at risk if ischemia continues without recanalization.

Source Content Treatment — Source Framing

The source lists r-tPA within 3–6 hours, anticoagulants for cardiac-source emboli, antiplatelets for recurrent TIA with atherosclerosis, neuroprotective/neuroregenerative agents, hyperbaric oxygen/ozone therapy; endovascular options include intra-arterial thrombolysis, mechanical thrombectomy for large-vessel occlusion in the first 6 hours with penumbra, balloon angioplasty and stenting; surgical options include carotid endarterectomy, EC-IC bypass and decompressive craniotomy/craniectomy for malignant infarction; rehabilitation includes physiotherapy, psychotherapy and social support.

Source Content Hemorrhagic Stroke — Intro

Types listed: intracerebral/intraparenchymal, subarachnoid, intraventricular and subdural hemorrhage. Etiologies include hypertension, AVM, aneurysmal rupture, cerebral amyloid angiopathy, neoplasm, coagulopathy, hemorrhagic transformation, venous thrombosis, sympathomimetic drug abuse, Moyamoya, sickle cell disease, eclampsia/postpartum vasculopathy, cerebritis and vasculitis.
