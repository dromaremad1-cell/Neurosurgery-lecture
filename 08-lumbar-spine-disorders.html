Complete Theory

Source Content Causes of Lumbar/Spinal Disorders

Congenital: arachnoid cyst. Traumatic: spinal fracture, traumatic disc prolapse. Inflammatory: pyogenic infection, TB. Neoplastic: extradural metastasis/lymphoma; intradural meningioma, schwannoma, neurofibroma, ependymoma of filum terminale. Degenerative: disc prolapse, spondylosis. Vascular: AV malformation.

Source Content Lumbar Anatomy & Intervertebral Disc

Five lumbar vertebrae. Vertebral body anteriorly, neural arch posteriorly, spinal canal between. Canal contains dura and mainly cauda equina. Intervertebral discs absorb shock and facilitate movement. Annulus fibrosus is strong collagen but thin posterolaterally, creating a weak point for herniation. Nucleus pulposus contains mainly water and proteoglycan.

Source Content Lumbar Disc Prolapse — Definition & Pathology

Nucleus pulposus herniates through a tear in annulus fibrosus. Typical age 30–50. Main cause of low back pain and sciatica in the source. 95% occur at L4-L5 and L5-S1. Posterolateral herniation causes radiculopathy; central herniation may cause cauda equina syndrome.

Source Content Clinical Picture — Symptoms

Low-back dull aching pain aggravated by movement and partly relieved by rest; sciatica in affected root distribution, worse with cough/sneeze and relieved by flexion of knee/thigh; dermatomal numbness/tingling; root-related weakness; flaccid bladder with retention and overflow in cauda equina; possible sexual dysfunction.

Source Content Clinical Picture — Signs & Root Localization

Back signs: loss of lordosis, local tenderness, limited movement, scoliosis, paravertebral spasm. Radiculopathy signs: motor weakness, dermatomal sensory change, reduced reflexes.

L3-L4 → L4: quadriceps weakness; medial leg/medial malleolus/medial foot sensory loss; impaired knee jerk. L4-L5 → L5: dorsiflexors/elevators weakness; lateral leg + dorsum foot + big toe sensory loss. L5-S1 → S1: plantar flexors/invertors weakness; posterior calf + lateral malleolus + lateral foot/sole sensory loss; impaired ankle jerk.

Source Content Nerve-Root Tension Tests

SLRT positive when passive elevation of fully extended symptomatic leg produces sciatica at <60°; significant in L4-L5/L5-S1. Augmented SLRT: dorsiflexion worsens sciatica. Crossed SLRT: raising asymptomatic leg produces sciatica in symptomatic leg. Femoral stretch/reverse SLRT: prone passive elevation causing anterior-thigh pain; significant for upper lumbar roots L2-L4.

Source Content Cauda Equina Syndrome

Large central lumbar disc herniation compresses cauda equina and needs urgent surgical intervention because delay may lead to permanent neurological deficit. Key manifestations: urinary retention with overflow, constipation, sexual dysfunction, bilateral LMN weakness of ankle movements/flail foot, possible hip/knee weakness, bilateral sensory loss including saddle hypesthesia, absent ankle/knee reflexes, bilateral positive SLRT.

Source Content Investigations

Plain X-ray AP/lateral and dynamic views may show disc-space narrowing, osteophytes, loss of lordosis, scoliosis, spondylosis. CT defines level/location, facet hypertrophy and canal dimensions and is better for bone lesions. MRI is the best diagnostic method for lumbar disc prolapse.

Source Content Treatment of Lumbar Disc Prolapse

Conservative treatment is for low-back pain/sciatica without neurological deficit, usually 4–6 weeks. Source lists bed rest 2–4 days on hard board mattress, activity modification, avoiding heavy lifting, analgesics/NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, weight reduction, lumbar support and physical therapy.

Surgical indications: cauda equina syndrome, persistent/progressive motor deficit, severe unremitting leg pain not relieved by conservative measures, and failed conservative treatment.

Source Content Lumbar Canal Stenosis

Congenital narrowing or acquired spondylotic degeneration. Disc-space collapse strains facets causing degeneration/hypertrophy. Compression may be from prolapsed disc, osteophytes, thickened ligamentum flavum or hypertrophied facet joint. Types: central, lateral recess, foraminal. Clinical picture: neurogenic claudication relieved by sitting/lying, bilateral neurological deficits often asymmetric, possible sphincter dysfunction; SLRT often negative.

High-Yield Summary

Lumbar disc prolapse

Most cases at L4-L5/L5-S1. Posterolateral → radiculopathy; central → possible cauda equina.

Root localization

L4 = quadriceps/medial leg/knee jerk. L5 = dorsiflexion/lateral leg+dorsum foot+big toe. S1 = plantar flexion/posterior calf+lateral foot/ankle jerk.

Tests

SLRT <60°, augmented SLRT, crossed SLRT, femoral stretch for upper lumbar roots.

Imaging

MRI best for disc prolapse; CT better for bony anatomy.

Emergency

Cauda equina = urgent surgery.

Lumbar stenosis

Neurogenic claudication relieved by sitting; central/lateral recess/foraminal types.
