Complete Theory

Source Content Lumbar Spondylolisthesis — Definition & Etiology

Forward slipping of one vertebral body over another, narrowing the spinal canal and possibly causing root compression. Common levels: L5-S1 and L4-L5. Etiology: congenital defective facet articulation; pars interarticularis fracture (isthmic); degenerative facet-joint process (degenerative spondylolisthesis).

Source Content Grades

Grade 1: <25%. Grade 2: 25–50%. Grade 3: 50–75%. Grade 4: >75% slipping of the upper vertebral body.

Source Content Clinical Picture & Investigations

Low-back/root pain, neurogenic claudication, usually bilateral neurological deficit, possible sphincter impairment in neglected cases. Investigations: AP/lateral and dynamic flexion-extension LSS X-rays; CT and MRI for diagnosis.

Source Content Treatment of Spondylolisthesis

Conservative treatment for low-back pain/sciatica without neurological deficit: bed rest 2–4 days on hard board mattress, external spinal support, analgesics/NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, weight reduction, physical therapy. Surgery includes decompression and fixation with screws/rods ± intervertebral cage for fusion.

Source Content Spinal Cord Compression — Overview

Compression may occur from cervicomedullary junction to conus at L1, producing UMN lesions. Below L1 there is cauda equina only, so compression produces LMN lesions. Early recognition and urgent treatment are required.

Source Content Pathology & Etiology of Compression

Source of compression: extradural 80%, intradural extramedullary 15%, intradural intramedullary 5%. Causes: congenital arachnoid cyst/syringomyelia; trauma including fracture, disc, hematoma; inflammatory pyogenic/TB; neoplastic metastasis/lymphoma/meningioma/schwannoma/neurofibroma/ependymoma/astrocytoma; degenerative disc/spondylosis; AV malformation.

Source Content Clinical Syndromes

Four syndromes: radiculopathy, myelopathy, radiculo-myelopathy and spinal shock. Pain is often the first/commonest complaint and may be local, radicular or central.

Source Content Motor / Sensory / Sphincter Localization

Cervical cord → spastic quadriparesis; dorsal cord → spastic paraparesis; conus → mixed UMN/LMN paraparesis; root compression → radiculopathy. Sphincter pattern: cord compression → urgency/frequency then incontinence; conus → mixed urgency/incontinence/retention; cauda equina → retention with overflow plus constipation/stool incontinence/impotence.

Source Content Spinal Cord Syndromes

Complete cord: complete motor, sensory and autonomic loss below lesion. Anterior cord: motor loss, often lower-limb prominent. Posterior cord: deep sensation loss causing sensory ataxia. Central cord: upper limbs more than lower, proximal more than distal, dissociated suspended sensory loss. Brown-Séquard: ipsilateral LMN signs at lesion, ipsilateral UMN + deep-sensory loss below, contralateral pain/temp loss below. Conus: mixed UMN/LMN legs. Spinal shock: acute loss of cord function with hypotension + bradycardia, differentiated from hypovolemic shock with tachycardia.

Source Content UMN vs LMN Table

UMN below lesion: minimal wasting, increased tone, clonus present, no fasciculations, hyperreflexia, absent abdominal reflex, extensor plantar, urgency/automatic bladder. LMN at lesion: marked wasting, decreased tone, no clonus, fasciculations present, hyporeflexia, abdominal reflex present, flexor/absent plantar, retention with overflow/flaccid bladder.

Source Content Radiological Investigations & Treatment

Plain X-ray may show bony destruction, pedicle thinning/interpedicular widening, posterior-body scalloping, widened foramen in neurofibroma, disc-space destruction in infection. CT gives bony detail; MRI gives soft-tissue detail; radioactive bone scanning for spinal metastases; metastatic workup may include CT chest/abdomen/pelvis, tumor markers and breast mammogram.

Spinal cord compression is a neurosurgical emergency. Treatment depends on cause; source states standard treatment is urgent surgery except selected malignant tumors where corticosteroids and radiotherapy may be used.

High-Yield Summary

Spondylolisthesis

Forward vertebral slip; common at L5-S1/L4-L5. Congenital, isthmic and degenerative causes; graded 1–4 by percentage slip.

Compression

Above L1 = cord/UMN pattern; below L1 = cauda equina/LMN pattern.

Source distribution

Extradural 80%, intradural extramedullary 15%, intradural intramedullary 5%.

Spinal syndromes

Complete, anterior, posterior, central, Brown-Séquard, conus and spinal shock.

Emergency

Spinal cord compression is a neurosurgical emergency; urgent treatment is emphasized.
