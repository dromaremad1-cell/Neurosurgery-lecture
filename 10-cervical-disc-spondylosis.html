Complete Theory

Source Content Epidemiology

Most commonly affected levels are C5-C6 and C6-C7. Usually occurs in middle age, 30–50 years.

Source Content Clinical Patterns

Posterolateral prolapse → nerve-root compression (radiculopathy). Central prolapse → cord compression (myelopathy). Root + cord compression → radiculomyelopathy.

Source Content Symptoms

Neck pain/stiffness; brachialgia worsened by neck extension and relieved by arm abduction; paresthesia/numbness along dermatome; weakness in longstanding compression; sphincter dysfunction in myelopathy.

Source Content Neck & Radiculopathy Signs

Loss of cervical lordosis, tenderness, limited movement, paravertebral spasm. Radiculopathy: root-specific weakness, dermatomal sensory change, decreased reflexes and root-specific upper-limb pain.

Source Content Root Localization Table

C3-C4 → C4: diaphragm. C4-C5 → C5: deltoid; biceps/brachioradialis reflex. C5-C6 → C6: biceps. C6-C7 → C7: triceps; triceps reflex. C7-D1 → C8: hand muscles.

Source Content Myelopathy Signs

Spastic quadriparesis, hyperreflexia below level, clumsiness/ataxic gait, sphincter disturbance.

Source Content Investigations

Plain X-ray may show loss of lordosis, disc-space narrowing, osteophytes, subluxation or other pathology such as osteolytic metastases. MRI is the best diagnostic technique. CT is more useful for bony details, osteophytes, congenital stenosis and OPLL.

Source Content Treatment

Conservative: bed rest, avoidance of heavy lifting, physical therapy, analgesics, muscle relaxant, neck collar. Surgical indications: brachialgia not responding to medical therapy, persistent root-related neurological deficit, and myelopathy. Procedures include anterior cervical microdiscectomy, ACDF ± fixation, posterior laminectomy ± foraminotomy, endoscopic posterior discectomy/foraminotomy and artificial disc replacement.

Source Content Cervical Spondylosis

Degenerative change with vertebral osteophytes, facet hypertrophy, ligament calcification and vascular stenosis. Sagittal canal diameter <12 mm may be associated with cord compression/myelopathy. Clinical picture: gradual neck pain/stiffness, radiculopathy, myelopathy, radiculomyelopathy, spastic lower-limb weakness with clonus and Babinski, weak hand grip and clumsiness. CT is best for bony anatomy; MRI is most accurate. Surgery: multiple ACDF ± fixation or posterior laminectomy ± foraminotomy.

High-Yield Summary

Core pattern

Posterolateral = radiculopathy; central = myelopathy; both = radiculomyelopathy.

Common levels

C5-C6 and C6-C7.

Roots

C5-C6 → C6; C6-C7 → C7; C4-C5 → C5.

Myelopathy

Spastic quadriparesis, hyperreflexia, gait clumsiness/ataxia, sphincter disturbance.

Imaging

MRI best for disc; CT better for bone/OPLL.

Treatment

Conservative first where appropriate; surgery for refractory brachialgia, persistent deficit or myelopathy.
